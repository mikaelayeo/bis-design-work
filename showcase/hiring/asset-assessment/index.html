1The brief

We care more about your reasoning, the questions you ask and the choices you make than about pixel-perfect polish.

The context

BIS Safety Software is a health and safety (EHS) platform used by construction, industrial and energy companies. Its Assets module is a register of a company's physical assets: trucks, excavators, forklifts, gas detectors, fire extinguishers and more. Each asset has details such as status, type and contacts, and is linked to the safety forms people complete for it, like daily inspections and maintenance checklists.

The people who use these screens are usually safety coordinators, fleet managers and site supervisors working on a desktop browser. They manage dozens to hundreds of assets, often under time pressure, and need to know which assets are safe to use today.

Your task

Redesign the experience across three screens so the user stories below are easy, clear and hard to get wrong.

  • Asset list (grid view only): browse assets by category, filter and search, switch between Active and Deactivated.
  • View asset: see and edit an asset's details, its pending and completed forms, and notes.
  • Add New: create a new asset.

You're free to change layout, hierarchy, components, copy and the flow itself. Keep it believable for a BIS product: use the design-system starter sheet as your base and add to it where you need to.

User stories

Required

Create an asset

As a safety coordinator, I want to add a new asset to the right category so that my team can start running inspections against it.

  • Start from the Asset list and end with the asset saved and easy to find.
  • Think about what's required vs optional, photo and status, validation, and what happens after saving.
Optional

Move an asset to another category. As a fleet manager, I want to move an asset that was filed in the wrong category (e.g. a forklift sitting under "Vehicles") so that the register stays accurate.

Deactivate an asset. As a safety coordinator, I want to deactivate a retired asset so that it moves to the Deactivated tab and stops showing up for my crews, while its history is kept.

3What to hand in

Two things: your Figma file and your notes doc.

Your Figma file

  • A new Figma file with a view link for us.
  • As many or as few pages as you like. One page is fine.
  • Show the create flow end to end, plus the optional stories if you get to them.
  • A prototype is welcome but not required.

Your notes doc

  • Use the template above and fill it in as you go, not at the end.
  • Questions for us: jot down what you'd ask our developers or product team. You won't get answers during the exercise, so note the screen it relates to or the assumption you made, and keep going.
  • The tools and AI you used, and what you used each one for.
  • A blank notes page for anything else you want to tell us.

Tools & AI

Design in Figma. For everything else, like research, plugins or prototyping, use whatever helps. AI is allowed at any step: research, ideation, copy, accessibility checks, generating a prototype, anything. We use AI every day on our team. Just list the tools and AI you used, and what for, in your notes doc.

Timebox: this shouldn't take you multiple hours. Aim for roughly 90 minutes to 2 hours. We'd rather see clear thinking on the create flow than a polished version of everything. If you run out of time, write down what you'd do next.

What we'll look at

  • The flow: is creating, moving or deactivating an asset clearer and safer than it is today?
  • Reasoning: can we see why you made each change? Did you research or reference anything?
  • Fit: does it feel like a BIS product, and would our developers know how to build it?
  • Communication: your notes, your questions, and how you explain your work.
