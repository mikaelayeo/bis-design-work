01

What this is

The map flows left to right. The first column is what can start today; every column after it waits on something to its left, and the lines converge on the MVP parent. Stages are computed from the dependency data, not hand-placed — adding a dependency re-lays-out the graph on the next load.

Hover or tab onto a card to trace its dependencies; everything unrelated dims and its direct neighbours are tagged before and after. Click, or press Enter, for the full detail panel.

The Scope tab is the same project one grain finer. The map stops at ticket-to-ticket — what blocks what. The scope matrix goes inside the MVP parent and asks which of its ten interface scope items each child actually gates, which is the question the map deliberately does not answer. Its column totals are the useful half: they say what each ticket unblocks, not what it waits on.

On the name. The initiative is Field Management. The filename, the source folder and this page’s URL keep their original spelling on purpose, so that no link in a ticket, a deck or a chat breaks — content renames, paths don’t. The SM- and S- key prefixes stay for the same reason, and they are no longer an abbreviation of anything: SM- means module-specific, S- means system-wide. Where Site Access appears, that is the existing licensed feature this module reuses the site object from.

02

Three lenses, not one

Clients group the same safety work three different ways. Some group it by site — a place, which owes things whether or not anyone is on it today. Some group it by job — an engagement with a start and an end, which is finished or it isn’t. Some group it by crew — a team that moves, and carries its obligations with it.

There is no single replacement for “site.” There are six, and no majority: compliance attaches to a job at 4 of 14 companies, to a place at 3, and to a person, an asset, a crew or a document at the rest. Any product that picks one container fails most clients structurally, which is why Site and Job are two kinds of one engine — SM‑01 — rather than one flexible object wearing two names. A Job has its own start, stage and close-out, and renaming a Site would give you a job that can’t end. Crew is the third lens and its own ticket, SM‑02 — a breakdown of the numbers, never a place a form counts toward.

Job used to be a ticket of its own, in a parallel build. It was folded into the work-context engine in the v2 pass and came into the MVP with it, which is why the map has no separate Job card — look for it inside SM‑01.

03

Two axes: tier and prefix

Tier is timing. The prefix is ownership. They are independent, and reading one off the other is the mistake the previous version of this page made. A system-wide ticket can be MVP-blocking; a module-specific one can sit in the backlog.

Split tickets — one ticket, several landing dates

Three tickets have parts that land at different times, and the dependency edges point at specific parts rather than at the whole ticket. So each slice is its own card, on a shared key stem with a lowercase suffix. A card carries a band repeating the stem and the ticket’s own name, identical on every slice, and each panel lists its siblings — because three cards in three different tier colours would otherwise read as three unrelated tickets.

The suffix is a slice letter, never a tier letter — lowercase precisely so it can’t be misread as one. In BIS PM a split ticket is still one ticket, so the same number will land on every slice of it.

04

⏳ Waiting on product leadership

Eight questions are deliberately open. They are not oversights and they are not disagreements — each one needs a call that hasn’t been made, and the core of the plan is locked for prototyping around them. Nothing on this page answers any of them, and a pending chip appears on every card a question lands on so the map can’t be read as if it had.

One of these makes a tier placement provisional: the training signal is shown in Tier A because that is where the scope register currently puts it, and whether training requirements stay in the MVP at all is one of the eight. Read that Tier A slot as pending, not as an answer.

05

Flagged, not fixed

A cross-ticket conflict goes here rather than inside a ticket panel, because it is a disagreement between tickets — it can’t be settled by whoever picks up either half alone. This is the record of those, and it is separate from the pending list above on purpose: a conflict is the plan contradicting itself, while a pending item is a question nobody has answered.

07

Keeping it current

This page never calls BIS PM. Claude does.

A browser page can’t reach an MCP tool — MCP runs on Claude’s side. So the page is a pure renderer, and refreshing means asking Claude to update the data file:

  1. Claude reads site-management-roadmap-data.json.
  2. For every ticket with a non-null pmTicketNumber, it calls mcp__pmMCP__getTicketDetails.
  3. It writes pmStatus, pmAssignee and pmUrl back into that ticket. Static fields are never touched.
  4. Newly created tickets get their pmTicketNumber filled in first — pmUrl is what makes a card link out.
  5. meta.lastRefreshed gets an ISO timestamp.
  6. Claude rewrites the JSON and the mirrored copy inside this HTML, between the ROADMAP-DATA markers.

Say “refresh the Field Management roadmap”. Full instructions are in REFRESH.md and in the header comment of this file. A split ticket is one ticket in BIS PM, so the same number goes on every slice of it.

One caveat. This page is published to a public repository. Filling in live BIS PM numbers and URLs puts internal links on the open web, which is a call nobody has made yet — so the live half stays empty until it is asked for explicitly.

Source of truth for every static field: _notes/scope/Site Managment/scope/ — the parent ticket file and one file per child. The folder’s spelling is legacy and deliberately unchanged, for the same reason this page’s filename is.
