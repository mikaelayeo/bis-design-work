1Scope

A buyer discovers a course on the marketing site (bissafety.ca), crosses into the software store (bissafety.app) to buy it, and never returns to marketing. This round redesigns the product page at the centre of that flow, and defines the responsive purchase experience end to end.

The flow

PagePropertyRole
store.htmlMarketingCourse catalogue. Prices removed. ✎
defensive-driving.htmlMarketingCourse group page. One umbrella page per topic instead of a page per course. +
defensive-driving-101.htmlSoftwareThe product page. Main subject of this PRD.
course-list.htmlSoftwareWhere Home leads. This is TKT-17024's work — proposed to stay as-is. ✓
Group pages have two valid entry points. Primarily they are entrance pages from Google or site search — that is where their SEO value is realised. But they are also reachable from the catalogue, and both paths are intended. Separately, clicking a specific course in the catalogue goes straight to the software product page rather than via its group page (Sean: "if I clicked forklift operator, I wouldn't go to the forklift landing page, it'd be straight to that course"). So the catalogue links to both group pages and individual products, depending on what was clicked.

Why this structure exists

The driver is SEO, not aesthetics. Roughly 330,000 monthly searches for WHMIS against ~3,000 for software, and the eCommerce store is not indexed at all — practically nobody lands there from search. Today every course needs its own marketing page, which creates mass duplication and, per Sean, about 14% of the current catalogue links to nothing because courses came down while their pages stayed up. Collapsing to roughly 90 to 100 stable group pages fixes both: the page never comes down, only the courses linked beneath it change.

In scope

  • Product page hero: breadcrumb, title, description, rating, course image, bulk pricing table.
  • Purchase card with Buy for Myself / For team, quantity stepper, and Add to cart.
  • A separate sticky mini card that appears once the hero card scrolls away.
  • Four tabs: About, Reviews, Certificate, FAQs.
  • Recommended Courses, always visible below the tabs.
  • Responsive behaviour to 360px, including the mobile sticky buy bar and its three states.
  • The one-way door: no navigation from software back to marketing.

Out of scope

  • Redesigning the software course list. course-list.html is TKT-17024's delivery and the proposal is to leave it as-is — it is a workable middle ground between the current store and where we are heading. Reproduced here so the loop can be clicked. See §8.9.
  • Cart, checkout, and post-purchase (tracked on #9587).
  • Admin Course List changes from this ticket (On Network column, Location filter) — unchanged by this round.
  • Pricing pages (Phase 2).

6.5 Edge cases and gaps

  • Long titles and descriptions. Store cards are a fixed 500px with no line clamp in the production design; the prototype adds a 5-line clamp. Real content will otherwise break the grid.
  • Zero or few reviews. The empty and low-volume state for the Reviews tab is undefined. A "top 10" list with three reviews will not read as credible.
  • Single-jurisdiction courses. The "and 3 more" pattern needs a rule when there is only one jurisdiction, or none.
  • Courses without volume pricing. Behaviour when a course has no bulk tiers is undefined; presumably the table is hidden.
  • Reduced motion. Honoured for the mini card fade.

6.6 Accessibility findings

Inherited from the current production store (course-list.html), not introduced by this design. Worth fixing while the area is open.
  • Search placeholder #807f7f on #feffff is roughly 3.5:1 — fails WCAG AA for input text.
  • The search field has no border while sitting on blue; boundary contrast is roughly 1.4:1.
  • The green CTA is the only green on an otherwise all-blue page.

In this design round: the mini card is visibility:hidden while concealed so it stays out of the tab order, and the assignment banner is inserted in DOM order before Add to cart so it is announced before the action.

6.7 Known deviations in the prototype

  • H1 font. Figma specifies Montserrat Bold; the prototype uses the system stack. Confirm whether Montserrat is being introduced.
  • Photography. Unsplash placeholders throughout.
  • Trust mark. Cropped from the full BIS lockup, as the isolated shield was not exported.
  • Course list styling. Deliberately reproduces the current production store, so it will not match the redesigned pages.

8Open questions

8.1 Can we show individual reviews at all? Blocking

The Reviews tab is directional, not committed scope. BIS captures and displays an aggregate rating and count today, but has never exposed individual review text or authors publicly.

Unknowns, all of which gate build:

  • Is review free text actually captured and retained in a queryable form?
  • Did reviewers ever consent to public display of their words, name or company? Compliance and legal question.
  • What is the moderation and reporting path for published reviews?
  • What does "top 10" sort by — most recent, most helpful, highest rated? Sorting by rating risks reading as cherry-picking.
  • What shows for a course with 0 to 3 reviews?

Owners: dev to confirm the data exists; compliance to confirm consent; PM to define ordering and the empty state.

8.2 Recommended Courses selection logic Blocking

This was the original ticket's open action item, assigned to Sachin Verma and Dan Macdonald, and it was never resolved — the feature was deferred instead. Reinstating it reopens the same question: same category, learner level, or "customers also bought"? The ticket also notes courses not launched in that company must not be recommended, which is a real filtering constraint.

Likely blocked by the same tagging gap as filtering (§6.4). Without product-level categories there is no basis for "same category" or "similar course" recommendations, leaving only raw purchase data. Recommend planning the tagging model once, to serve filtering and recommendations together.

8.3 Does the price follow the volume tiers?

The purchase card shows a single price while the bulk table advertises three. If a buyer sets quantity to 10, does the displayed price and cart total drop to $24.99 per seat automatically? Undefined, and it directly affects cart logic (see B-7).

8.4 FAQ content and scope Awaiting Sean

Content is not approved. The three questions in the prototype are placeholders with locally written answers. The ticket's curated set (Making a Purchase, Buying for Someone Else, Virtual Proctoring, Bulk Purchasing, Purchase Orders, Finding Course Codes, Refunds) predates this design round and needs revisiting.

Open decision: do we want a unique FAQ set per product or group, or one standard set shown everywhere?

The trade-off, to frame the decision:

Design note: the ticket already floated an FAQ page toggle ("off by default") to control which FAQs appear in the store, which points toward the hybrid model. If a decision is needed quickly, the standard set unblocks this design round at no build cost, with per-product overrides deferred.

8.6 Physical products are unaccounted for Gap

This design assumes every product is an online course. BIS also sells physical products — the eCommerce FAQ set includes "Physical Products, Getting Started" and "Managing Physical Product Orders" — and none of that was covered in the Sean review or in this design.

Almost every element of the purchase card is course-specific and meaningless for a physical item:

  • Duration ("70 minutes"), Certificate Included, OHS Compliant and jurisdiction validity have no physical equivalent.
  • "Buy for Myself / For team" and seat language assume assignable licences, not shipped goods.
  • Bulk pricing per seat would need to become per unit.
  • Physical goods need shipping, stock and delivery messaging. Sean noted courses are "infinite, no stock" — the opposite is true here.
  • The Certificate tab and much of the About tab do not apply.

Decision needed: does the product page need a physical-product variant, or are physical products explicitly out of scope for this redesign? If in scope, this materially changes the purchase card.

8.7 Filtering — now working, but tagging-dependent + Built

Sean was explicit that filtering must drive the list: "when you filter this, some things move, your shopping list should match what your filters are." This now works in the prototype. Also built from review feedback: the filter panel is sticky so a buyer can switch category while scrolling results (Mahi's suggestion), and course length now appears on the group cards, which Sean called out as a missing decision criterion and which matters when courses overlap heavily.

Behaviour as implemented, for QA:

  • OR within a group, AND across groups. Selecting CMV and Winter returns courses that are both.
  • Sections with no matching courses hide entirely, so there are no empty headings.
  • A live count ("Showing 3 of 9 courses") and a Clear all filters action appear once any filter is active.
  • A no-results message shows when nothing matches.
This is prototype-only logic. Production filtering depends entirely on the product-level tagging in §6.4, which does not exist yet. The chips here are backed by hand-authored tags on each card.

8.8 Course comparison Idea

Raised in the review and received well. Because courses within a group can overlap heavily, a compare view would do real work in helping buyers choose. Not designed and not committed. Parking it here so it is not lost.

8.9 Software course list — proposal: leave it as-is Position taken

This screen is TKT-17024's delivery, and the proposal is to keep it. It is not far from the current store, and it sits reasonably close to where we want to end up, so it works as a middle ground while the product page redesign is scoped separately.

Practically this means:

  • No rework of the course list is requested as part of the product page redesign.
  • 17024 can continue to release this screen on its own schedule.
  • Buyers will cross from a redesigned product page into a slightly older-looking list. That is an accepted trade-off for now, not an oversight.
