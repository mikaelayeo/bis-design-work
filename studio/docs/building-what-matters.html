Building what matters

Our sprint items are good. But are they the right ones? A proposal to add real discovery to how we decide what to build — with Park Paving as the evidence.

The pattern

We're solving things.
Maybe not the right things.

Right now, our sprint backlog is built from support tickets, light client conversations, and team intuition. That's not nothing — but it's reactive. We're answering what's loudest, not what's most painful. And we don't really know which is which until we go look.

🎯

Backlog tickets come from what surfaces — support, sales calls, internal hunches. Not from observing real workflows.

🔍

We only learn how complex a feature really is once design starts — by then the sprint is committed.

📐

Designs go straight to dev without a client checkpoint. We build, ship, and find out later if it fit.

The evidence

One site visit. Five critical findings.
None in the June sprint.

On May 15, Mika and Aymeric visited Park Paving — 850 workers, 18,000 FLHAs last year, BIS client since late 2022. The goal: investigate their main struggles with the app that disrupt even their most basic workflows.

Workers were so used to uninstalling and reinstalling the BIS app daily to fix performance issues that it looked natural to them. The app crashed because 314 assets were being auto-synced to all 850 devices. Their operations were halted by our services.

These are the highest-severity findings from that one visit:

Critical
Asset auto-download crashes the app

314 assets syncing to 850 workers' devices simultaneously. App becomes unclickable, then crashes. Workers reinstall daily.

Critical
Download-first architecture, not cloud-first

Everything is stored locally on first interaction. Should be cloud-first, with users opting in to offline access per file.

High
Duplicate FLHA submissions

Worker submitted form once. System didn't recognize it. Worker ended up with 9 duplicates and was still flagged as incomplete.

High
Admin gets kicked to home page after editing pending forms

Strenuous review process. Submit takes admin out of context. Needs to be grouped by date for proper triage.

High
Bulletin sign-off is choppy

Users just need to read a document and sign it on the spot. Current flow is multi-step and confusing.

High
Reporting dashboard numbers don't match reality

Floyd abandoned the BIS dashboard. He tracks compliance in a separate HSE Excel sheet. Manually.

Medium
20+ Likert scale rows, each opens a modal

Form completion is exhausting on touch devices. Volume × friction = abandonment.

The closest match is "Optimize Large Form Performance Using FlashList" — which helps, but doesn't address the root cause (asset auto-download architecture). Nothing else in the June sprint touches what we observed.

Why this happens

We don't have a discovery track.

Most mature product teams run two tracks in parallel: Discovery (what should we build, and why?) and Delivery (build it well). At BIS, we have one — delivery. Discovery happens accidentally, when someone makes time for it.

That means the backlog gets filled with whatever's easiest to scope, not what users struggle with most. Anita scopes what we know about. We don't always know what we don't know.

Intercom

Quarterly "customer immersions" — designers and PMs go on-site. Every major feature must touch a real user before dev. Result: 40% fewer post-launch revisions.

Atlassian

Remote prototype sessions for every significant feature. Research is treated as a cost-reduction mechanism, not a luxury.

Shopify

Designers and PMs work shifts at real merchant stores. Features designed from that experience have measurably higher adoption.

The cost

One visit pays for itself a hundred times over.

A sprint at BIS costs tens of thousands in dev, QA, and PM time. A site visit is a few hours and a drive. If discovery prevents one wrong-direction sprint per year, it pays for the entire research program.

Closing the loop

Show clients designs before dev builds them.

Discovery answers what to build. Client design review answers are we building it right? It's a 30-minute Figma walkthrough with a real user, before dev touches the ticket. We've never done this at BIS. It's standard at every B2B SaaS company that scales.

The format:

  • 01. Design complete in Figma — clickable prototype, not pixel-perfect.
  • 02. PM picks 1–2 client contacts who use the feature daily.
  • 03. 30-minute walkthrough. Three questions: does this solve your problem? what's confusing? what's missing?
  • 04. Major findings get fixed before dev. Minor ones go into a "known follow-ups" list.
  • 05. Design locked → dev starts with field-validated signal.
For BIS specifically: We already have Floyd and Dan at Park Paving. For any feature touching FLHAs, assets, or offline workflows, a 20-minute Figma call with Floyd will tell us more than a month of internal debate.

The full pipeline

What discovery-informed development looks like.

Not a full overhaul. We're adding two upstream steps and one gate. Everything else stays the same.

Discovery
Field signal feeds the pipeline

Quarterly site visits, support pattern reviews, client calls. New input. Outputs: problem statements, not tickets.

Design
Designer works 2 sprints ahead

PM flags candidates 4–5 weeks before sprint. Designer has runway to investigate complexity before committing.

Client Review
30-min walkthrough with 1–2 real users

For major features only. New step. Validates the direction before dev. Catches workflow mismatches.

Readiness Gate
Ticket cannot enter sprint without:

Design done · Scope updated to match design · QA wrote AC · Dev estimated. New rule. Not ready = next sprint.

Delivery
Sprint planning → build → QA → release

The part we already do well. Dev gets ready tickets. QA has AC. Sprint planning is just planning.

The four asks

What I'd like us to agree on.

01

Design runway: candidates flagged 4–5 weeks ahead

Not a locked commitment — just a soft list. So I can start investigating complexity before sprint commitment. Without this, every sprint is a complexity-surprise lottery.

02

Readiness gate before sprint entry

If a ticket doesn't have design done, scope aligned, AC written, and estimate by T−1 week → it moves to the next sprint. No exceptions. Protects QA, backend, and dev from inheriting half-formed work.

03

Client design review for major features

A 30-min Figma walkthrough with 1–2 trusted clients before dev starts. Especially for features touching workflows we've heard about in the field. Start with Floyd at Park Paving.

04

One discovery slot per sprint cycle

Reserve one Mika-ticket per cycle for something that came from field discovery — not from the backlog. Quarterly site visits feed this. Without it, we're forever reactive.

One thing to try

A June → July pilot.

We don't have to change everything at once. Here's the smallest version that tests the model:

Pilot — for the July sprint cycle

Test the full flow on one ticket. See if it feels different.

  • Anita and Mika pick one July ticket to run through the new pipeline. Ideally something tied to a Park Paving finding.
  • Anita flags the candidate by May 27. Mika starts design week of June 2.
  • Design locked by June 16 (T–2 weeks before July sprint planning).
  • 30-min Figma walkthrough with Floyd at Park Paving, week of June 17.
  • QA writes AC against the validated design. Dev estimates. Ticket enters July sprint as the cleanest one we've ever shipped.
  • After the sprint: did this ticket have fewer surprises? Did QA have what they needed? Was the client happier with what shipped?

If the answer is yes — we roll it out to more tickets. If not — we learn what didn't work and adjust. Either way, we move forward with data, not vibes.

The bigger picture

This isn't a process complaint.
It's product strategy.

Sprint structure isn't the real conversation. The real conversation is: how do we make sure what we build is what users actually need? Right now, we're guessing pretty well. With one site visit, we found out we were also missing the biggest issues entirely.

Discovery, runway, and client review aren't extra work. They're the difference between a product that grows because clients love it, and a product that grows because clients haven't left yet.

Let's build what matters.
