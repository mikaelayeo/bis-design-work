Out of the
Black Box

How a 13-week initiative to build a faster wireframe tool turned into a pipeline that strengthens the front and back of our design process — and what it taught me about where design's value actually lives.

📍 In one breath
  • I set out to automate wireframes. I ended up fixing where the time actually goes.
  • Design has been a black box — ticket in, finished file out, no one can react in between.
  • The fix: two windows into that box — a review on the way in, and on the way out.
  • Not about automating design. About making space to think before we design.

What 13 weeks taught us ⭐

🛠️

Automate the right part

The value isn't drawing the wireframe — it's deciding if the problem is real and the approach is right.

🧩

Three artifacts, one field

A ticket is forced to be a problem, a scope, and a spec at once. So design does forensic work every time.

📐

Complexity is discovered, not predicted. Designing the thing is what reveals how big it really is.

🎨

Not a UI artist

💵

Building the wrong thing costs orders of magnitude more than finding out first.

🪞

Review at escalating fidelity, eng + QA in early — not one big reveal at the end.

The pivot

"My first idea was simple: an automatic wireframe generator. Feed it a ticket, get wireframes out — faster output, just like the goal said."
▶ OPEN — Ticket 18984 (comment + generated wireframe)
A good starting point — but not usable for production. The tool can draw; it can't decide if it's the right thing.
skill: draft-wireframeagent: Lia
"The expensive, valuable work isn't producing the wireframe. It's figuring out whether the problem is even real, and whether this is the right solution. Automating the wireframe automates the wrong part."
So the project changed: not "make design faster" — "make sure design is solving the right problem before it starts."

The problem · value

Design is the scope

The three-artifact problem: a ticket is asked to be a problem, a scope, and a spec — three artifacts, three authors, collapsed into one field.
"Scope gets written before design — but design is the detailed scope. Complexity is discovered, not predicted. Offline and SSO looked medium. Designing them revealed they were large."
This is exactly why we built the front of the pipeline — to separate the problem from the scope from the spec, instead of asking one ticket to be all three.

The problem · the proof ~1.5 min

Park Paving

"One 3-hour site visit surfaced at least five issues more critical than our entire June sprint. Workers were uninstalling and reinstalling a paid app every single morning."
That's not a UX nitpick — it's churn risk. One sprint costs $30–60k; that visit cost a drive and a few hours.
Why it's a business problem: captive users hide costs — bad FLHA data is a safety problem, not just friction. Buyers aren't users. Competitors like Procore and SafetyCulture win on field UX.
"The cost of building the wrong thing is orders of magnitude higher than the cost of finding out first."

The solution · why the gates matter ~5 min

Two windows into the box

🧨

Concept Direction — entry

Before polished design: problem, approach in plain steps, rough greyscale sketches, open questions tagged to reviewers. "Is this the right approach?" Catches wrong turns when changing course is cheap.

🧩

Proposed Changes — exit

At finalization, before dev: exactly what's changing, behavior + state level. QA turns it into test cases; dev builds from it. "Confirm exactly what we're building."

▶ OPEN — Ticket 19998 (Concept Direction, first real use)
The banner + workflows table + greyscale wireframes + tagged open questions. Reviewers get auto-routed to the concept and the wireframes.
skill: pm-brief-route-reviewers
You're not inventing this: mature orgs review as a thread, not a gate — Shape Up, Amazon working-backwards, Stripe/Figma/Linear. The roughness is the point — greyscale tells reviewers to critique direction, not styling.
⚠️ The honest caveat — say it before anyone asks
"Right now I author both gates and I'm the only reviewer — so today it's me reviewing me. The windows fix the timing problem; real independent review only starts with a second person. That's the argument for this being a team discipline, not my personal QA."
